What the U.S. is doing in AI literacy

Many people talk about AI literacy as a forecast for the coming years. In the United States, it has already become public policy: there is a presidential decree, funding, an official skills framework, and programs underway in schools, at work, and in small businesses.

2presidential executive orders (Apr/2025)
60+organizations committed to education
5areas in the official competency framework
20,000+students in the 2026 national challenge
56planned state hubs

1 · Timeline

In just over a year, an entire policy

  • Apr/2025
    Executive order on AI education for young people. Creates a White House task force, a national AI challenge for students, and partnerships with companies for K–12 education. A second executive order, on skilled trades, sets the goal of 1 million learners.
  • Jun 2025
    Commitment to young people. More than 60 organizations, including Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Amazon, Apple, and Meta, commit to investing in AI education. In the same month, about US$ 84 million go to the states to expand apprenticeship programs.
  • Jul 2025
    AI academy for teachers. The teachers' union AFT creates a national AI training academy with Microsoft, OpenAI, and Anthropic (US$ 23 million).
  • Jul 2025
    AI Action Plan. Makes workforce training a condition for the pillars of innovation, infrastructure, and leadership.
  • Aug/2025
    National challenge rules published, and guidance from the Department of Labor: public job retraining funds can pay for AI literacy courses.
  • Sep 2025
    Anthropic joins the commitment, with US$ 1 million for cybersecurity education and open courses on "AI fluency".
  • Jan 2026
    Small businesses in Congress. The House approves a bill requiring the federal agency that supports small businesses (SBA) to train them in AI. The Senate has yet to act. Microsoft launches a free AI credential for teachers.
  • Jan to Jun 2026
    The challenge is happening. Registration until Jan 20, state champions in March, regional champions in April, and national ceremony at the White House on June 9, with more than 20,000 students participants.
  • Feb. 13, 2026
    AI Literacy Framework from the Department of Labor: 5 content areas and 7 teaching principles. It is voluntary, not mandatory.
  • Mar/Apr 2026
    “AI-Ready America” program from the NSF, the federal science agency, in partnership with the Departments of Labor and Agriculture and the small business support agency: one coordination hub per state and territory.
  • Apr. 13, 2026
    AI priority in education funding. The Department of Education begins prioritizing projects that train teachers, expose students to AI, and provide certifications in its grant programs. Effective May 13.
  • May to Aug 2026
    Federal bill to free up K–12 education funding for AI curricula (House in May, Senate in August). In June, the Department of Labor opens up job retraining funds for people who lose their jobs to AI.
  • Jul 2026 →
    First round of hubs (10 hubs, proposals until Jul. 16, 2026; selection not yet announced as of 09/28). Then, 20 in Jan. 2027 and 26 in Jul. 2027.
2 · The three fronts

School, work, and small business

🏫 Schools

  • National AI challenge for elementary and high schools and teachers. Students propose or build an AI solution to a problem in their own community. It has state, regional, and national stages.
  • Company-led teacher training: Anthropic’s open curriculum (Creative Commons), US$ 150 mi from Google, 10,000 educators through Amazon by 2028, and more than US$ 20 mi from Meta.
  • Education grants start giving preference to projects with AI.

👷 Workers

  • Official framework AI literacy framework, detailed below.
  • Job retraining funding can pay for AI courses for unemployed and displaced workers.
  • Professional learning: a goal of 1 million learners, with funding distributed to every state.

🏪 Small businesses and communities

  • AI-Ready America: AI literacy and practical skills, technical assistance for small businesses and municipalities adopting AI, and internships and real-world projects.
  • One hub per state: US$ 1 mi per year for 3 years (plus one possible additional year), 56 hubs in total.
  • Includes the rural extension, bringing AI literacy to the field.
3 · The competency framework

What counts as "knowing how to use AI"

The Department of Labor framework (Feb 2026) is the most useful document in the entire package: it explains what to teach and how to teach it. It focuses on generative AI.

5 content areas

1Understand how AI works

2Explore uses of AI

3Direct AI effectively (know what to ask)

4Evaluating AI responses: check facts, see if anything is missing, find flaws in the logic

5Use AI responsibly: safety and data

7 teaching principles

  • Learning by doing, with real tasks, comparing responses and improving with each attempt
  • Develop complementary human skills: judgment, communication, and problem-solving
  • Teach within the context of the profession
  • Create pathways for keep learning
  • Design for change quickly, because the tools keep changing
  • Care for the prerequisites (access, basic digital skills)
  • Prepare those who teaches and supports
The phrase that sums it up: AI should help the worker, not replace responsibility of them for the outcome.
4 · 2026 updates

What changed this year

🏆 The challenge in numbers

  • More than 20,000 students from all 50 states, the capital, Puerto Rico, and 49 overseas military schools.
  • Award-winning projects: homework help, bullying prevention, support for people with visual impairments. The award-winning teachers teach AI within the subjects they already teach.
  • The ceremony took place amid a growing backlash against screens and AI in schools.

🗺️ The states

  • 77 bills in 27 states about AI in the classroom only in 2026.
  • Examples: Maryland (AI policy in each school district and AI literacy), Idaho (literacy standards, teacher training, AI does not replace teachers), Oklahoma (AI only with teacher supervision).
  • California already requires AI literacy to be included in math, science, and history curricula.

🏛️ Congress and government

  • No federal law AI literacy framework approved by September 2026. There are projects for schools and small businesses; the latter have already been approved by the House.
  • New Department of Labor funding to retrain workers affected by AI (Jun 2026).
  • The government trains its own employees: an official 2026 AI course and training with Stanford and Princeton.

📊 What the research shows

  • 6 out of every 10 teachers used AI during the 2024–25 school year; 3 in 10 use it every week and save about 6 hours per week (Gallup, Jun. 2025).
  • 26% of teenagers use ChatGPT for school assignments, twice as many as in 2023 (Pew, Jan 2025).
5 · Critical reading

What works and what raises concerns

✅ What’s real

  • There is a decree, funding, deadlines, and a published framework.
  • The national challenge actually took place, with the final in June 2026.
  • The framework is good: practical, tied to professions, and focused on evaluating responses.
  • The focus on small businesses and rural areas reaches people who are usually left out.

⚠️ What raises concerns

  • Patchwork: the programs are scattered, and the results depend on each state taking action.
  • Small amount of funding for a country this size. US$ 1 mi per year per state is too little.
  • Big tech companies set the direction: companies train teachers using their own tools. Who decides how AI enters the classroom?
  • Few safeguards privacy and outcome evaluation in the grant programs.
  • The Department of Education is being dismantled, with the risk of losing the people who know how to run the programs.
  • AI literacy without digital literacy: cuts to digital inclusion funding affect the local organizations that would provide the foundation (Brookings).
  • The stated motivation is the geopolitical rivalry. Literacy is treated as part of a race.
6 · Lessons

What we can apply here

1. Course checklist

Use the 5 areas as a checklist: does every AI course cover all five? Areas 4 (evaluating responses) and 5 (using AI responsibly) are the ones most often missing.

2. Teach through the profession

AI for lawyers, accountants, healthcare professionals, and store owners, each with their own examples, teaches more than a generic lesson.

3. Community challenge

The challenge format (solving a real problem in your neighborhood or city with AI, in stages) works in a school, group, or online community.

4. Open material

Open-license AI curricula already exist that can be adapted and translated for Brazilian teachers.

5. Small business first

In the U.S., small businesses and local governments have their own dedicated initiatives. In Brazil, these are the groups that need the most support and receive the least.

6. Neutrality

Teaching without relying on a single provider is an advantage over training funded by big tech companies.

What universities and companies offer for free

Curated AI literacy courses and materials, with a focus on free options. "100%" = entire course at no cost; "audit" = take the course for free and pay for the certificate; "restricted" = free only to a specific group, such as U.S. teachers. "?" = unconfirmed. Check the link before recommending it.

Best options for Brazil today: IBM SkillsBuild (100% free, free certificate, in Portuguese), AWS Skill Builder (partly in Portuguese), LinkedIn/Microsoft (Portuguese subtitles), and Google Cloud Skills Boost (lessons in Portuguese).
